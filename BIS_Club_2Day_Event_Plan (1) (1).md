# **1\. Overview & Objectives**

The BIS Club 2-Day Event is designed to build awareness among students about the role of standards (Bureau of Indian Standards) in everyday life, while encouraging creativity, teamwork, quick thinking, and public speaking through a mix of competitive and fun activities.

**Event Goals**

* Introduce students to the concept and importance of standards through hands-on and creative activities.

* Encourage teamwork, leadership, and healthy competition among participants.

* Provide a platform for creative expression (art, drama, rangoli) alongside academic rigor (quiz, debate).

* Recognize and reward outstanding performance across all events.

**Target Participants**

Open to all club members / students, organized in teams for group events and individually for others (details under each event).

| Day / Time | Day 1 — 10:30 AM – 12:30 PM |

To creatively communicate a standards/quality-related message through visual art, in either traditional or digital medium.

## **4.3 Debate**

| Day / Time | Day 1 — 2:00 – 3:30 PM |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Team Size** | Teams of 4 |

**Objective**

To develop critical thinking and public speaking skills while discussing real issues connected to standards, quality, and regulation.

**Materials Required**

* Mic & timer/stopwatch

* Bell/buzzer for time signals

* Score sheets for judges

* Topic chits (if topics are drawn on the spot)

**Procedure — How to Conduct**

1\. Teams register in advance; topics are shared in advance, per organizer preference.

2\. Each round: For-team speaks first (3 min), followed by Against-team (3 min).

3\. 1-minute rebuttal each, followed by 1–2 minutes of open floor/cross questions if time allows.

4\. Timekeeper signals at 30 seconds remaining (single bell) and time-up (double bell).

5\. Judges score each speaker independently, then tally to declare the winning side per round.

6\. Repeat for all registered pairs/teams; finalists (if bracket format) debate again in the last slot.

**Suggested Topics / Themes**

* Should mandatory quality certification apply to all consumer products?

* Voluntary standards vs mandatory standards — which drives better compliance?

* Is self-regulation by industry enough, or is third-party certification necessary?

* Should schools include 'standards & quality awareness' as a subject?

**Rules**

* Strict adherence to time limits; judges may deduct marks for overtime.

* No personal remarks; arguments must stay on-topic.

* Teams must present logical, fact-based arguments — dramatics without content will be marked down.

**Judging Criteria**

* Content & argument strength (35%)

* Rebuttal & response to counterpoints (25%)

* Clarity, fluency & confidence (25%)

* Time management (15%)

## **4.4 Treasure Hunt**

| Day / Time | Day 1 — 3:30 – 5:00 PM |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Team Size** | Teams of 6 |

**Objective**

To promote teamwork and problem-solving while reinforcing knowledge about standards through fun clues and tasks placed around the campus.

**Materials Required**

* 5 sealed clue envelopes placed at checkpoints around campus

* Clue sheet master copy with the answer key

* Small task props (e.g., a BIS-marked product to find, a puzzle piece, a QR code)

* Stopwatch, finish-line marker, walkie-talkies/phones for volunteers at checkpoints

**Procedure — How to Conduct**

1\. Organizing team hides 8–10 clues in advance at fixed checkpoints (library, lab, garden, notice board, etc.), each leading to the next.

2\. Teams are briefed on rules and given the first clue at the starting point, released at staggered intervals (2–3 minutes apart) to avoid teams following each other.

3\. Each clue is a riddle, question on standards, or simple task (e.g., 'find a product in this room carrying the ISI mark and note its number').

4\. Volunteers stationed at each checkpoint verify the team has solved the clue correctly before handing over the next one.

5\. The final clue leads to the 'treasure' (a symbolic box/certificate marker) at the finish point.

6\. Time taken by each team from start to finding the treasure is recorded; fastest correct completion wins.

**Rules**

* Teams must stay together at all times; splitting up leads to disqualification.

* No skipping checkpoints or checking other teams' clues.

* Out-of-bounds areas will be announced beforehand.

* Total time capped at 60 minutes; incomplete teams are ranked by checkpoints cleared.

**Judging Criteria**

* Fastest correct completion (primary criterion)

* Number of checkpoints cleared (tie-breaker)

* Adherence to rules / fair play

# **6\. Day 2 — Event Procedures**

## **6.1 Rangoli Competition**

| Day / Time | Day 2 — 7:00 – 9:00 AM |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Team Size** | Teams of max 4 |

**Objective**

To showcase creativity and teamwork through traditional rangoli art, optionally themed around standards/quality symbols (e.g., ISI mark motifs, Hallmark, colour-coded safety symbols).

**Materials Required**

* Rangoli powder/colours (multiple colours per team)

* Chalk for outline (optional)

* Measuring tape to mark out equal floor space per team

* Water, diyas/decorative items (optional, if allowed)

* Camera for judging documentation

**Procedure — How to Conduct**

1\. Each team is allotted an equal marked floor space (e.g., 4x4 ft) in advance.

2\. Teams bring/collect their own rangoli materials; organizers may supply a base colour kit.

3\. 2 hours given to complete the design from start to finish — no pre-drawn outlines allowed unless outline-only stage is permitted by rules.

4\. Judges walk around at the end to evaluate all designs together (not sequentially, to keep comparison fair).

5\. Photographs are taken of every entry for record and display.

**Suggested Topics / Themes**

* Free theme, OR themed: 'Standards in Nature', 'Safety Symbols', 'Unity through Quality'

**Rules**

* Only powder/petals/natural colours allowed — no paint on permanent flooring unless pre-approved.

* Team must stay within the allotted space.

* All 4 members should be seen contributing (judges may informally note participation).

**Judging Criteria**

* Creativity & design (30%)

* Colour combination & neatness (25%)

* Relevance to theme, if themed (20%)

* Teamwork visible during execution (15%)

* Completion within time (10%)

## **6.3 Quiz**

| Day / Time | Day 2 — 9:00 – 10:30 AM |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Team Size** | Individual |

**Objective**

To test participants' knowledge of BIS, standards, general science, and current affairs in a fast-paced quiz format.

**Materials Required**

* Quiz question bank (prepared in advance, 4–5 rounds)

* Buzzers or hand-raise system

* Scoreboard (physical or projected)

* Mic & timer

**Procedure — How to Conduct**

1\. Teams are seated separately; a scoreboard is displayed/visible to all.

2\. Round 1 — General Awareness on BIS/Standards (rapid fire, direct questions to each team in turn).

3\. Round 2 — Science & Technology (buzzer round, open to all teams).

4\. Round 3 — Visual/Audio round (identify logos, marks, or products related to standards).

5\. Round 4 — Rapid Fire bonus round for top-scoring teams to widen or close gaps.

6\. Running score is tallied after each round; final scores decided the top 3 teams.

**Suggested Topics / Themes**

* BIS & the ISI mark

* Hallmarking of jewellery

* Indian and international standards bodies (ISO, IEC, etc.)

* General science & current affairs

* Consumer rights & product safety

**Rules**

* No conferring with other teams.

* Buzzer precedence strictly followed; wrong answers on buzzer may attract negative marking (organizer's discretion).

* Quizmaster's decision on ambiguous answers is final.

**Judging Criteria**

* Purely score-based — highest cumulative points across all rounds determines ranking.

## **6.4 Mimes**

| Day / Time | Day 2 — 11:00 AM – 12:30 PM |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Team Size** | Teams of 4–6 |

**Objective**

To creatively convey a message related to standards, quality, or safety through silent (non-verbal) performance.

**Materials Required**

* Stage/performance space

* Background music system (optional, no dialogue)

* Simple props as needed by teams

* Timer/stopwatch

**Procedure — How to Conduct**

1\. Each team registers a topic in advance or draws one on the spot (organizer's choice).

2\. Teams get 3–5 minutes for their performance — strictly no dialogue or spoken words; only expressions, gestures, and optional background music/sound effects.

3\. Performances happen back-to-back with brief set-up gaps between teams.

4\. Judges score immediately after each performance based on the criteria below.

**Suggested Topics / Themes**

* Importance of quality checks before buying a product

* Consequences of using substandard/duplicate goods

* Journey of a product getting BIS certified

* Safety standards saving a life

**Rules**

* Absolutely no spoken words or sound effects that substitute for dialogue.

* Performance must stay within the time limit (a warning bell at 30 seconds remaining).

* Content must be appropriate and non-offensive.

**Judging Criteria**

* Clarity of message conveyed without words (35%)

* Expression & body language (25%)

* Creativity & originality (25%)

* Team coordination & timing (15%)

## **6.5 Learning Science via Standards**

| Day / Time | Day 2 — 1:30 – 3:00 PM |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Team Size** | Teams of  2–4 |

**Objective**

An event where participants build models based on specific topics and explain the underlying science behind them.

**Materials Required**

* Projector/screen for presentation

* Sample products for demonstration (if hands-on)

* Simple test-kits or demo experiment materials (e.g., testing water purity, checking a product's ISI mark authenticity)

* Handouts/worksheets summarizing key concepts

**Procedure — How to Conduct**

1\. Resource person or senior student leads a short interactive talk (15–20 min) on how standards are developed and tested scientifically.

2\. Live demonstration of 1–2 simple tests (e.g., checking a claimed vs actual quantity, basic quality checks) to show 'science in action'.

3\. Participants split into small groups for a short hands-on activity or worksheet applying what was demonstrated.

4\. Quick recap / Q\&A to close the session, reinforcing key takeaways.

**Suggested Topics / Themes**

* How products get tested before certification

* Basic quality control experiments

* Reading and understanding a standard mark/label

* Science behind everyday safety standards (food, electrical, toys, etc.)

**Rules**

* Participants must actively engage in the hands-on portion for full session credit (if graded/certified).

**Judging Criteria**

* Not competitively judged — participation-based; optional short quiz/worksheet can be used to award a 'Best Learner' recognition.
