About the Event

Develop critical thinking and public speaking skills while discussing real issues connected to standards, quality, and regulation. Teams argue for or against assigned topics in a structured debate format.

Learning Science via Standards

Pressure Cooker — Science Behind Everyday Safety
10:45 AM – 12:30 PM
FormatTeam of 2–4
VenueMain Auditorium
Duration1 hr 45 min
Prize₹20,000+ Total
“Don't just display a product. Explain the science that makes it work.”
Build it. Demonstrate it. Explain the science. Connect it to Standards.

About the Event

Participants will build and demonstrate an educational prototype inspired by the official BIS Learning Science via Standards initiative.

For this edition, the selected topic is the Pressure Cooker. The objective is not simply to display a pressure cooker — participants must demonstrate how scientific principles influence the design, operation, efficiency and safety of the product, and explain how standardisation ensures quality.

The project must connect: Science → Engineering → Product → Safety → BIS Standard

Safety Requirement

For safety, participants must not pressurize a homemade prototype. The prototype is intended for educational demonstration only. If demonstrating pressure/temperature experimentally, use commercially manufactured laboratory-safe equipment, a low-pressure demonstration setup, simulation, LEDs, gauges, transparent non-pressurized models, or prerecorded/controlled demonstrations.

Required Prototype — Cutaway Pressure Cooker

Build a visually impressive cutaway pressure cooker model that clearly shows its internal mechanism. Use transparent/acrylic sections or a cutaway design so judges can see inside.

① Cooker body & Lid
② Sealing gasket & Locking mechanism
③ Steam outlet & Steam path
④ Pressure regulator / Weight
⑤ Safety valve mechanism
⑥ Water/food chamber & Heat source

The Science Behind the Pressure Cooker

Pressure & Boiling Point

Water boils when its vapour pressure equals the surrounding pressure. Inside the cooker, trapped steam raises internal pressure — which raises the boiling point, allowing cooking above 100°C.

Ideal Gas Law
PV = nRT

P = Pressure · V = Volume · n = Amount of gas · R = Gas constant · T = Temperature (Kelvin)

Gay-Lussac's Law
P / T = constant

At constant volume, as temperature increases, pressure increases proportionally — directly explaining what happens inside a sealed pressure cooker.

Why Cooking is Faster

Heating → Steam generation → Pressure rise → Higher boiling temperature → Faster cooking. The elevated temperature dramatically accelerates heat transfer to food.

Where BIS Standards Come In

IS 2347 — Domestic Pressure Cookers
Bureau of Indian Standards — the standard governing safety and quality of domestic pressure cookers in India

Participants must explain how standards relate to product quality and safety, connecting each component of the cooker to its engineering purpose and safety requirement.
