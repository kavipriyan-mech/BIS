Learning Science via Standards: LED Bulbs (IS 16102)
Stall Team Sheet

1. Physical Model Setup

Set up a side-by-side comparison board showing an Incandescent Bulb (60W) and an LED Bulb (9W) connected to identical AC power sources through individual switches and inline ammeters/multimeters.

🛒 Parts & Materials List

  • Baseboard: 1.5 ft × 1 ft wooden/acrylic mounting board
  • Bulb 1: 60W Incandescent bulb (Tungsten)
  • Bulb 2: 9W B22 LED Bulb (BIS certified, IS 16102)
  • Holders: 2 B22 batten lamp holders
  • Measurement: Digital multimeter or dual AC ammeter modules
  • Thermal Demo: Non-contact IR thermometer OR temperature indicator strips on each bulb dome
  • Wiring: Insulated copper wire, 2 ON/OFF rocker switches, 3-pin plug top

⚡ What the Visitors See & Feel

  1. Brightness: Both bulbs emit similar luminous flux (~800 lumens).
  2. Power Draw: Incandescent draws ~0.26 A (~60 W), while LED draws ~0.04 A (~9 W) — ~85% less energy!
  3. Heat Generation: After 2 minutes, incandescent surface exceeds 100°C (waste heat); LED heat sink remains safe (~40°C).

2. Visitor Q&A Script (Tying to IS 16102)

Q1: Why does the 9W LED produce as much light as a 60W Incandescent bulb?
Answer: Incandescent bulbs create light via resistive heating of a tungsten wire — 95% of energy becomes heat, only 5% light. LEDs generate light directly through electroluminescence in a semiconductor (GaN), converting electricity directly into photons without heating up to thousands of degrees.
Q2: What is IS 16102 and why should buyers look for the ISI mark on LED bulbs?
Answer: IS 16102 is the Indian Standard for self-ballasted LED lamps. BIS tests certified bulbs for:
  • Luminous Efficacy: Ensures true lumen output per watt (prevents fake rating claims).
  • Lumen Maintenance: Guarantees brightness won't drop dramatically after a few hundred hours.
  • Electrical Safety: Prevents short circuits, insulation breakdown, or shock hazards.
  • Harmonics & Power Factor: Prevents electrical noise on your home wiring grid.
Q3: What happens if someone buys an uncertified, cheap non-ISI LED bulb?
Answer: Cheap uncertified LEDs often use poor heat sinks and unrated driver capacitors. They flicker (causing eye strain), overheat quickly, lose brightness within weeks, or fail prematurely — creating e-waste and potential fire/shock hazards.

3. Stall Team Checklist

  • ✅ Test all wiring with a multimeter before connecting to power source.
  • ✅ Ensure all open wire terminals are heat-shrink insulated or taped.
  • ✅ Keep the IS 16102 standard summary sheet displayed prominently.
  • ✅ Invite visitors to observe the multimeter readings live!
